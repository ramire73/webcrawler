students across 18 faculties at five separate campus locations — four in Edmonton and one in Camrose — plus other unique locations across the province. Learn about each of the unique campuses that make up the fifth largest university in Canada. north-campus.html North Campus north-campus.html Our original campus that blends the historic with cutting edge: University of Alberta's North Campus north-campus.html is located in the province's capital of Edmonton, on the edge of the North Saskatchewan River valley. It covers 50 city blocks, with abundant green spaces among its 150 buildings. Research Stations Outside of our five main campuses, the U of A maintains research stations https://afns-labs.ualberta.ca/Research-Stations/ across the province. These include Edmonton Research Station, also known as the University of Alberta Farm at South Campus, as well as Mattheis Research Ranch, Roy Berg Kinsella Research Ranch, St. Albert Research Station, and Augustana Miquelon Lake Research Station https://www.ualberta.ca/en/augustana/research/amlrs/index.html . Territorial Acknowledgement The University of Alberta, its buildings, labs and research stations are primarily located on the territory of Néhiyaw (Cree), Niitsitapi (Blackfoot), Métis, Nakoda (Stoney), Dene, Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) and Anishinaabe (Ojibway/Saulteaux), lands that are now known as part of Treaties 6, 7 and 8 and homeland of the Métis. The University of Alberta respects the sovereignty, lands, histories, languages, knowledge systems and cultures of all First Nations, Métis, and Inuit.

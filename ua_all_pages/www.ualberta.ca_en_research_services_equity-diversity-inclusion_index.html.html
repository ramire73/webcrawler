          EDI in Research Knowledge Hub

          Welcome to the U of A equity, diversity and inclusion (EDI) in Research service space! 

          At the University of Alberta, we are committed to advancing EDI in research. By incorporating EDI principles, we harness a broader range of ideas, expertise, and perspectives, resulting in more innovative thinking and stronger outcomes that better reflect our diverse world. EDI considerations also help researchers create projects that have a positive impact on society as a whole. We take pride in being among the first signatories of the Government of Canada's Dimensions Canada charter, which aims to increase EDI within post-secondary institutions and the research ecosystem. We are also currently a member of the Dimensions Recognition Program. See the EDI in Research News section below for more information on how EDI fits into U of A’s commitment to Access, Community, and Belonging.

          OFFICE OF THE VP (RESEARCH) EFFORTS

          The Office of the Vice-President (Research) is leading and collaborating on various institution-wide initiatives to further integrate EDI in U of A's research activities.

          Accessibility in Research Support Fund

          We know that researchers living with disabilities or who are neurodivergent are often forced to use research grants to cover accessibility needs. This fund is available to cover expenses for accessibility in research and knowledge mobilization activities.

          Learn more about the Fund »

          Dimensions Recognition Program

          EDI remains an important framework to guide care and responsibility in research under the U of A's goals of increasing access, providing community, and fostering belonging. The Tri-Agency Dimensions Recognition Program aims to identify and eliminate obstacles and inequities impacting post-secondary research by trainees, postdoctoral fellows, and full-time and part-time academic and non-academic staff.

          Territorial Acknowledgement

          The University of Alberta, its buildings, labs and research stations are primarily located on the territory of Néhiyaw (Cree), Niitsitapi (Blackfoot), Métis, Nakoda (Stoney), Dene, Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) and Anishinaabe (Ojibway/Saulteaux), lands that are now known as part of Treaties 6, 7 and 8 and homeland of the Métis. The University of Alberta respects the sovereignty, lands, histories, languages, knowledge systems and cultures of all First Nations, Métis, and Inuit.
